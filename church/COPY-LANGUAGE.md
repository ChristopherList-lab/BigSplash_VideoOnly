# Church language guide

How Big Splash talks to pastors and church staff. Use the operational vocabulary heavily. Use scripture-flavored language sparingly and never as a sales hook.

## The rule

Church people use two vocabularies. **Operational church words** (guest, connection card, next steps, fall launch) are safe and expected. **Scripture and theology language** (harvest, the one, stewardship) is powerful in small doses and cringeworthy when a vendor overuses it. One scripture-flavored line per page or email, never more.

Never use scripture to justify buying something. "Be a good steward, buy our package" reads as manipulation.

## Say this, not that

| Say | Not | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Guests, first-time guests, families, neighbors | Leads, prospects, traffic | Pastors don't see people as leads |
| Plan your visit | Landing page, opt-in | Their own vocabulary for the same thing |
| Next step, connection card, welcome team | Funnel, CTA, conversion path | Funnel is our word, not theirs |
| Came back a second week | Retention, repeat rate | Plain and human |
| Your mission field, your zip code, a 10-minute drive | Target audience, market, geo-fence | Mission field is theirs and it's specific |
| The people moving in around you | Demographic, segment | Texas suburbs are full of new arrivals |
| Fruit, what it produced | ROI, return on souls | "Souls" plus money will cost you deals |
| Stewarding your budget | Maximizing spend, efficiency | Stewardship is a core church value |
| Campaign, invitation | Ads (in pastor-facing copy) | Ads sounds commercial; campaigns and invitations don't |
| Message, sermon, teaching | Content, assets | "Content" reduces the sermon to material |
| Serving churches | Clients, accounts | Service language fits the culture |
| Church staff, comms director, tech team | Marketing department | Matches how churches are actually organized |

Words to avoid entirely: souls as a metric, "conversion" for anything but salvation (say "signups" or "arrivals"), "selling the gospel," "customers," "growth hacking," "scale your church."

## Operational vocabulary worth using

**Guest journey:** first-time guest, second-time guest, plan your visit, connection card, next steps, guest services, welcome team, first impressions team, greeters, foyer or lobby, kids check-in, assimilation, back door (people leaving), side door (events that bring people in).

**Church calendar:** fall launch or fall kickoff, series launch, vision Sunday, baptism Sunday, Christmas Eve services, Advent, Holy Week, Easter, VBS, back to school, small groups launch, Friend Day, Homecoming (Baptist), preview services and grand opening (church plants), core team or launch team.

**Church roles:** senior pastor, lead pastor, executive pastor, comms director, creative director, tech or production team, elders, deacons (Baptist), board, finance committee, church planter.

**Church-world terms:** unchurched, de-churched, prodigals, seekers, multisite, campus launch, church plant, sending church, discipleship pathway, invite culture, the local church.

## Scripture-flavored lines, used sparingly

Pick one per page or email. Keep it light and never preachy.

- "The harvest is plentiful. Your staff is not." (Matthew 9:37, and a fair description of a church with one comms person.)
- "Your Jerusalem has a zip code." (Acts 1:8, the local mission field.)
- "We help you find the one." (Luke 15, the lost sheep. Works for a report that names every guest.)
- "Faithful with the message. Fruitful with the invitation."
- "Every number on this report is a name."
- "Your members' invitations still work best. We give them something worth sharing."

Avoid: "return on souls," "souls per dollar," "harvest season sale," anything pairing salvation with money or urgency.

## Headline options in church language

1. "Fill Sunday with new guests. Know every family who came."
2. "Your city is growing. Make sure it knows you're here."
3. "The harvest is plentiful. Your staff is not."
4. "Your pastor preached for 40 minutes. How many new neighbors heard it?"
5. "From your sermon to their seat."
6. "Every number on your report is a name."
7. "Stop being your town's best kept secret." (Note: ChurchCandy uses this idea. Don't copy their wording.)
8. "We count the people who walked in."

## Denominational notes

- **Southern Baptist (SBC, SBTC, Texas Baptists):** revival, outreach, missions, deacons, business meeting, associational. Cooperative Program giving matters. Decisions often move faster than in mainline churches.
- **Non-denominational:** guest experience, growth track, next steps, campus, creative team. Most comfortable with marketing language.
- **Methodist and Global Methodist:** connectional, conference, charge conference, administrative council. Churches that left the UMC lost conference communications support, which is a real opening.
- **Church of Christ:** more reserved about production language. Lead with hospitality and clarity, not video polish.
- **Hispanic congregations:** many Texas churches are bilingual. Offer Spanish captions and Spanish ad versions. That's a differentiator almost nobody offers.

## Tone

Write like a church member who knows how a Sunday actually runs, not like an agency. Short sentences. Name things pastors touch: the parking lot, the kids' check-in line, the Wednesday email. Never claim the campaign saves anyone. The campaign gets a family through the door. What happens after that is the church's.
