The Word · 2 Chronicles 5:13–14 · KJV

“It came even to pass, as the trumpeters and singers were as one, to make one sound to be heard in praising and thanking the LORD; and when they lifted up their voice with the trumpets and cymbals and instruments of musick, and praised the LORD, saying, For he is good; for his mercy endureth for ever: that then the house was filled with a cloud, even the house of the LORD; so that the priests could not stand to minister by reason of the cloud: for the glory of the LORD had filled the house of God.”

Set the scene
≈ 1 min

Solomon dedicates the temple. Before a note is played, every priest has sanctified himself, and nobody waits for his shift (v.11). Levites in white linen hold cymbals, psalteries and harps, with 120 priests on trumpets (v.12). The sound goes up as one, and God fills the house so thick the priests can’t stand.

Point 01 · ≈ 3 min

Your craft is
an offering.

“with the trumpets and cymbals and instruments of musick”v.13
  • The Levites brought their best instruments and dressed for the moment. God received their skill as worship.
  • Our instrument is the console. A fader move nobody notices is still an offering. God sees the hands on the desk.
  • Creativity counts. A reverb throw on the last chorus, or pulling the band back when the room starts to sing, is worship with your ears.
  • Excellence honors God. It isn’t about impressing people. Practice nights like tonight are part of the offering.
Ask the roomName one part of your role that feels invisible. God sees that part.

Point 02 · ≈ 3 min

The booth is on
the worship team.

“as one, to make one sound to be heard”v.13
  • The singers couldn’t make one sound alone. Voices and instruments had to arrive together. Making one sound heard is the booth’s job description.
  • You are as much a part of the worship team as the person holding the mic. The room hears the band through your hands.
  • Nobody waited for their shift (v.11). FOH, monitors, lyrics, lights and stream serve as one team.
  • Unity looks like early call times, serving the song over our taste, honoring the band in how we talk to them, and owning mistakes fast.
Say it plainlyIf the room worships, the booth worshiped too.

The response · ≈ 2 min

Consecrated
first.

“all the priests that were present were sanctified”v.11

The priests got their hearts ready before the music started. The glory came to a prepared people, not a perfect soundcheck. A line check matters. A heart check matters more.

Together · Weekly

The team prays together every week we serve. Sunday call time, before line check.

On your own · All week

Time in the Word and in prayer. You can’t give the room what you haven’t received.

Close in prayerPray over the team: for one sound, and for hearts that stay close to Him. “For he is good; for his mercy endureth for ever.”

Hands on · Drums on the DM7 · ≈ 15 min

Every drum.
Same order.

Build every drum channel in this order, every time. On the DM7 we run the gate in Dynamics 1 and the compressor in Dynamics 2.

01

Gain

Head amp

Set it on the loudest song. Hits land with headroom to spare. Faders fix balance, not gain.

02

HPF

High-pass filter

Cut the low end the drum doesn’t make. Clears mud and stage rumble before it stacks up.

03

Polarity

Ø button

Flip snare bottom. Check kick in against kick out, and overheads against the snare. Keep the fatter one.

04

Gate

Dynamics 1

Toms first. Kick only if it’s messy. Never on hi-hat or overheads.

05

EQ

4-band PEQ

Cut before you boost. Sweep a narrow boost to find the ugly frequency, then pull it down.

06

Comp

Dynamics 2

Control, not squash. A slower attack lets the stick through. Then route to the drum group.

Know the mic first

A scooped kick mic like the Audix D6 already cuts about 8 dB at 600 Hz. Cut it again and the kick goes hollow.

Hands on · The drum group · ≈ 10 min

One kit. One fader.

DCA · Remote control

Rides the kit

Moves the drum faders together. No audio passes through a DCA, so you can’t compress it. Use it to ride the whole kit through a song.

Group bus · The glue

Shapes the kit

A mix bus every drum flows through. One place to compress and EQ the whole kit so it hits like one instrument.

Glue settings
Ratio2:1 to 4:1
Attack10–30 ms
ReleaseFast or auto
Reduction2–3 dB

Group EQ stays gentle: a touch at 60–100 Hz, a touch of air near 10 kHz.

Routing on the DM7

Drum channels → Drum group bus → Stereo

Turn the drum channels’ direct send to Stereo off. Send them both ways and the kit doubles up and jumps in level every time you touch the group.

Level up · Parallel smash

Send the kit to a second bus and crush it: 8:1 or more, fast attack, 8–12 dB of reduction. Tuck it under the group for big choruses.

On the multitrack · Build it together

Build the kit from
the ground up.

01

Faders down. Drum group fader at unity.

02

Kick alone. Gain, HPF, EQ, comp. Then take it out of solo.

03

Snare top and bottom. Flip the bottom and keep the fatter setting.

04

Overheads. The picture of the kit. Check them against the snare.

05

Toms. Gate them so fills sound full with no ring between hits.

06

Hi-hat. Bring it up until you hear the time, then stop.

07

Group. Two to three dB of glue, then ride the DCA against the band.

Listen for

Kick and bass lock into one note. The snare cuts through without fighting the vocal.

Cymbals sit behind the vocal, never on top. Solo to fix, un-solo to judge.
